# San Miguel Watershed Water Quality Dashboard

An interactive map and charting dashboard of water quality data for the San Miguel River watershed in southwest Colorado. Each result is compared against the state water quality standards that apply to the stream or lake segment where it was collected. Built by the San Miguel Watershed Coalition (SMWC).

**Live site:** https://sanmiguelwatershed.github.io/water-quality/

> Draft for board and stakeholder review. Exceedance flags are screening indicators, not formal attainment determinations (see [Limitations](#limitations)).

## Methods

### Compiling results
- Each record keeps its original organization, method, fraction (dissolved / total recoverable), and source file.
- **Units are normalized at build time:** °F to °C; total phosphorus µg/L to mg/L; consistent metal units.
- **Implausible values are excluded from charts**, e.g. stream temperatures above 40 °C or below −1 °C (°F and °C values mislabeled as each other).
- **pH:** values outside 2–12 are excluded as implausible. Naturally acidic sites such as Iron Bog (pH 3.2–4.0) are real and kept. The CDSN characteristic "pH measured at" is the lab's temperature during the pH measurement (about 20–23 °C), not pH, and is excluded.
- **Specific conductance:** the legacy SMWC database (`WQ_DBASE_2010`) labels conductance µmhos/cm, but most of its values are actually mS/cm (median 0.3 versus about 400 µS/cm elsewhere). Values under 10 from that file are multiplied by 1,000; its genuine µS/cm values (50–2,300) are unchanged.
- **Single-record corrections** for clear data-entry errors are listed in `DATA_CORRECTIONS` in `build_app_data.py` and applied at build time; the master workbook is left as received. Currently: Waterfall 2006-06-22, where pH and conductance were entered in each other's columns.
- **Suspect records** are excluded via `DATA_EXCLUSIONS` in `build_app_data.py`, pending review against field sheets. Currently: Fall Creek 2007-06-15 (pH 3.13) and Bear Creek 2014-05-22 (pH 10.57).
- **QA/QC log:** every excluded, corrected, or suspect record, plus systematic and site-level issues, is listed in `data_qaqc_log.csv` in the Drive folder (not in this repository) for follow-up.
- **Sample counts:** each chart states the total samples collected and how many were not detected. A *Show non-detects* checkbox (on by default, remembered per browser) can hide the non-detect markers.
- **Non-detects** are results the lab reported as "not detected", "non-detect", "below detection/reporting limit", or "<". They are plotted as triangles at the reporting limit (or the method detection limit if no reporting limit is given) and never flagged as exceedances. The tooltip shows "not detected (< limit)".
- **Estimated values:** results flagged `J` or "present below quantification limit" were detected, but below the level the lab can quantify reliably. They are plotted as normal results, labeled "estimated" in the tooltip.
- **Why non-detects can sit above detected results:** a non-detect is drawn at its reporting limit (e.g. < 0.05 mg/L), while estimated detections from the same lab can be lower (e.g. 0.011 mg/L). Reporting limits also differ between labs and years. The true value of a non-detect is anywhere between 0 and the limit.
- **Zero values:** metals and inorganic chemistry results reported as exactly 0 with no detection flag are treated as non-detects with no reported limit. There are about 25,000 such records, mostly Colorado River Watch 2002–2011. They are plotted at 0 as non-detects and excluded from medians.

### Assigning sites to segments
Every site is assigned to a CDPHE assessment unit (e.g. `COGUSM03b_A`). The unit identifies the Regulation 35 segment (`COGUSM03b` → segment 3b).
- **Stream sites:** nearest stream assessment unit (spatial join in UTM 13N).
- **Lake and reservoir sites:** any site that falls inside a lake polygon is assigned to that lake's segment (e.g. Cone, Gurley, and Trout Lake → `COGUSM20_A`; Miramonte → `COGUSM20_B`).
- **303(d) status** is attached from the same layers and shown in each site's header, split by Regulation 93 listing type:
  - *303(d) impaired:* not attaining; a cleanup plan (TMDL) is needed.
  - *TMDL in place:* impaired, with an approved cleanup plan.
  - *Monitoring & Evaluation:* possible non-attainment, data not conclusive; not an impairment.
- The sites sheet columns `Ag`, `AQLife`, `Rec`, `WS` are **303(d) assessment status codes** (F = fully supporting, N = not supporting, I = insufficient information, X = not assessed). They are not use classifications. Use classifications come from Regulation 35.

### Site types
Each site has a `site_class` in the sites sheet (editable; initially assigned from site IDs, names, and location types):

| Class | Sites | Standards applied |
|---|---|---|
| `surface` | Streams, rivers, lakes, reservoirs | Reg 35 segment standards (below) |
| `wwtf` | Wastewater treatment facility effluent (`…-EFF` sites) | Regulation 85 effluent limits |
| `mine_discharge` | Adits, portals, tunnel discharges | None (data only) |
| `groundwater` | Springs and seeps | None (data only) |

Non-surface sites keep their nearest segment as the *receiving* segment for reference. They are excluded from the Longitudinal Profile and don't show the segment's 303(d) status.

### Wastewater effluent (Regulation 85)
WWTF effluent is compared with the Reg 85 §85.5(1)(a)(iii) limits for existing domestic wastewater treatment works:

| Parameter | Rolling 12-month median | 12-month 95th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| Total phosphorus | 1.0 mg/L | 2.5 mg/L |
| Total inorganic nitrogen (as N) | 15 mg/L | 20 mg/L |

- **TIN calculation:** total inorganic nitrogen is calculated per sample date as ammonia + nitrate + nitrite. Non-detects count as zero.
- **Flagging:** each result is flagged if the median or 95th percentile of all samples in the preceding 12 months exceeds the limit.
- **Per-plant status:** a plant whose reported effluent flow has exceeded 1.0 MGD (currently only Telluride) is shown as *delayed, likely applicable after 12/31/2027*. All others are shown as *possibly exempt*, pending confirmation of design capacity.
- **Benchmark only:** these limits are not yet in permits here. The San Miguel basin (HUC 14030003) is a low-priority watershed, so limits cannot be added before 12/31/2027 (§85.5(1)(a)(ii)). Facilities ≤ 1.0 MGD design capacity or owned by disadvantaged communities are excluded (§85.5(1)(a)(i)).

### Standards
Standards for surface water sites come from each segment's table in **Regulation 35, Appendix 35-1** (the edition in the Drive folder, currently effective 12/31/2024).
- Per Reg 35 §35.6(1), each table lists only the most stringent standard for each parameter. That value is the standard used here.
- **The segment's use classifications** (aquatic life class, recreation, agriculture, water supply) determine its table values. Examples: chronic arsenic of 0.02 µg/L (Water + Fish) on water supply segments, 7.6 µg/L (fish ingestion) where there is no water supply use, and 100 µg/L (agriculture) on segments 6a and 6b.
- **"TVS" (table value standard) entries** are resolved with the hardness equations in **Regulation 31 Table III**. Hardness is capped at 400 mg/L CaCO₃.
- **Arsenic exception:** the 0.02 µg/L total recoverable chronic arsenic standard (Water + Fish) is kept as the standard of record but is not charted or flagged. It is below lab detection and natural background, and is under revision through 2029. Charts and flags use 10 µg/L instead: the EPA drinking water MCL and Reg 31 Table III footnote 13 attainment threshold for water supply segments.
- **Site-specific values** in the Reg 35 tables are used as written, e.g. chronic dissolved zinc of 190 µg/L in segments 3a, 3b, 6a, and 6b.
- **"WS" entries** for iron, manganese, and sulfate use the Reg 31 values (300 µg/L, 50 µg/L, 250 mg/L dissolved). Per §35.6(2)(b), these apply where water supply is an actual use; the dashboard applies them to every water supply segment.
- **Uranium** on water supply segments: 16.8 µg/L, the health-based end of the 16.8–30 µg/L standard in §35.5(3).
- **Where a cell lists two values** (e.g. manganese chronic "TVS/WS"), both are stored and charts show the stricter one.
- **Idarado Record of Decision zinc target** (150 µg/L dissolved; State of Colorado v. Idarado Mining Co., 1987) is shown separately for segments 3a and 3b.
- Total phosphorus, total nitrogen, and chlorophyll *a* are not flagged. EPA ecoregional reference conditions are shown as context only.

### Hardness-based (TVS) exceedances
For each metal result, hardness is taken from the first available of:
1. The same site and date.
2. The same site within ±7 days (average).
3. The site's median hardness.
4. The segment's median hardness.
5. A basin fallback of 192 mg/L.

The TVS is calculated at that hardness and the result is flagged if it exceeds it. The hardness source is recorded for each result. Chart reference lines use the segment median hardness.

### Temperature
- **Tiers:** each segment's temperature tier comes from its Reg 35 table (CS-I, CS-II, WS-II, CL, CLL). Segments 3b and 4b have custom seasonal values listed in their tables.
- **Values:** tier values come from Reg 31 Table I. Acute applies to the daily maximum (DM); chronic applies to the maximum weekly average temperature (MWAT).
- **Seasonal comparison:** each reading is compared only to the standard in effect on its date.
- **Charts:** acute and chronic are each drawn as one stepped line that changes at the season boundaries. Dots are colored by in-season exceedance (instead of shaded bands).
- **Grab samples:** most readings are single grab samples, so these comparisons are a screening indicator only.

### Dissolved oxygen
DO standards are minimums. The year-round chronic minimum is flagged. The 7.0 mg/L spawning minimum is shown for reference but not flagged, because spawning periods are not known for each site.

### Charts
- **Site View** draws each standard as a dashed line, with a legend explaining each line type (acute, chronic, spawning minimum, drinking-water screening level, Reg 85 limits, Idarado ROD target, pH range). Shaded bands show attaining, chronic exceedance, and acute exceedance zones.
- **Only the binding (strictest) value is drawn** for each standard type. The standards panel lists every standard that applies.
- **Parameter View** draws a standard line when every selected site's segment has the same standard for that parameter. Where segments differ, the lines are omitted and the view lists which parameters are affected.
- **Hardness-based (TVS) lines** are not drawn in Parameter View, because they vary by sample; exceedance flags still use each sample's hardness.

### River distance (Longitudinal Profile)
`build_app_data.py` reads the distances from `stream_distances.csv` on every build. Before Nov 2026 they were added once by hand, and later rebuilds dropped them, which emptied the Longitudinal Profile.

**Site medians include non-detects.** A non-detect's true value is below the lab limit, so it ranks below every detected value; no number is substituted. If the middle sample is a non-detect, the site's median is shown as a triangle labeled "not detected", with the sample and non-detect counts in the tooltip.

`compute_stream_distance.py` downloads the San Miguel flowline network upstream of its mouth from the USGS NLDI API. It builds a directed graph and calculates cumulative distance from the mouth. Each site is then snapped to its nearest flowline. Sites near confluences should be reviewed, since they can snap to the wrong branch.

### Stream discharge (flow)
SMWC field discharge is computed with the USGS **mid-section method** (USGS TWRI Book 3, Chapter A8):

Q = Σ vᵢ · dᵢ · wᵢ

- vᵢ: velocity at station i.
- dᵢ: depth at station i.
- wᵢ = (tᵢ₊₁ − tᵢ₋₁) / 2: width of station i, from the tape positions on either side (half-widths at the two banks).

Tape, depth, and velocity readings are in feet and ft/s, and results are in cubic feet per second (cfs). Calculations are recorded in `2025_Flow_spreadsheet.xlsx`.

## Build scripts

Run these from the Drive folder (`SMWC_Water_Quality_App/`) with Python 3. Required packages: `pandas`, `geopandas`, `openpyxl`, and `pdftotext` (poppler) for `build_standards.py`.

### `build_standards.py`
Rewrites the `standards` sheet of `master_water_quality_6.xlsx` from the Regulation 35 PDF.
- Extracts every Appendix 35-1 segment table and records its use classifications and each acute/chronic cell. The result is saved to `reg35_segments.json`.
- Writes one row per table cell for every segment used by a site. TVS cells are stored as hardness equations (Reg 31 Table III); temperature rows carry their season window.
- Keeps the Idarado Record of Decision rows and existing Reg 93 (303(d)) status notes.
- Reads the regulation edition date from the PDF and records it with each row.

```bash
python3 build_standards.py
```

### `build_app_data.py`
Builds `app_data.json` for the web app from the master workbook.
- Assigns sites to stream or lake segments and attaches 303(d) status; applies Reg 35 standards to surface sites and Reg 85 limits to WWTF effluent.
- Normalizes units, handles non-detects, and filters implausible values.
- Builds per-segment standards, computing TVS values at segment median hardness.
- Flags acute, chronic, and Idarado exceedances for every result (per-sample hardness for TVS; in-season standard for temperature).

```bash
python3 build_app_data.py
```

### `compute_stream_distance.py`
Writes `stream_distances.csv` (site, flowline COMID, distance from mouth in km, snap distance). Uses only the Python standard library plus the USGS NLDI web API.

```bash
python3 compute_stream_distance.py
```

### `shp_to_kmz.py`
Converts a zipped shapefile to KMZ.

```bash
python3 shp_to_kmz.py path/to/layer.zip
```

## Updating the dashboard

1. **New data:** append results to the `activities` sheet (and any new sites, with coordinates, to `sites`) in `master_water_quality_6.xlsx`.
2. **New regulations:** when CDPHE updates Regulation 35, replace `Water Quality Regulations/5 CCR 1002-35.pdf` and run `build_standards.py`.
3. **Rebuild:** run `build_app_data.py`.
4. **Check locally:** run `python3 -m http.server 8000` in the folder and open http://localhost:8000.
5. **Publish:** commit `index.html` and `app_data.json` and push to `main`. GitHub Pages redeploys automatically.

## Limitations

- **Non-detect markers are drawn at the reporting limit.** A non-detect's true value is anywhere from 0 up to that limit, so a triangle at a high reporting limit (e.g. Colorado River Watch selenium at 5.4 µg/L) can sit above other labs' detections. Plotting non-detects along the zero line, with the limit in the tooltip, is under consideration and has not been adopted yet.
- **What a triangle covers depends on the lab.** Labs that report `J` estimates (CDPHE, SMWC's contract lab) report values between the detection and reporting limits as estimated dots, so their triangles mean below detection. Labs that don't report estimates (mostly Colorado River Watch) report anything below the reporting limit as not detected, so their triangles also include results that were present but not quantifiable.
- **Reporting limits differ by lab, method, and era.** SMWC's recent data has the lowest limits for most metals (e.g. arsenic 1 µg/L, selenium 0.25 µg/L). River Watch and older state data are typically 5–10 µg/L. Some state reporting limits are set conservatively high relative to what the instrument can detect.
- **USGS "detected, not quantified" results** (390 records, no value reported) are all from stations not shown in the app, so none are included.

- **Screening only.** Exceedance flags are a screening tool. Formal attainment assessments follow CDPHE's listing methodology (e.g. 85th percentiles, two-month geometric means for *E. coli*, continuous data for temperature).
- **Arsenic.** The 0.02 µg/L arsenic standard is below what current laboratory methods can detect, so nearly every detected arsenic result exceeds it. A statewide temporary modification for permitted dischargers (§35.6(2)(c), through 12/31/2029) does not change the in-stream standard.
- **Approximate hardness.** Hardness-based standards are approximations when same-day hardness is not available.
- **Unassigned sites.** Some sites lack coordinates or a segment assignment and are not shown until those are added to the `sites` sheet.

- **5 CCR 1002-31 (Regulation 31):** Basic Standards and Methodologies for Surface Water. Tables I–III (physical/biological, inorganic, and metal standards).
- **5 CCR 1002-35 (Regulation 35):** Classifications and Numeric Standards for the Gunnison and Lower Dolores River Basins. Appendix 35-1 segment tables.
- **5 CCR 1002-85 (Regulation 85):** Nutrients Management Control Regulation. Effluent limits and monitoring for wastewater dischargers.
- **5 CCR 1002-93 (Regulation 93):** Colorado's Section 303(d) List of Impaired Waters.
- **Record of Decision, Idarado Mining and Milling Complex:** State of Colorado v. Idarado Mining Co., Civil Action 83-C-2385, March 1987.
- **USGS TWRI Book 3, Chapter A8:** Discharge Measurements at Gaging Stations.
- **EPA ecoregional nutrient criteria:** EPA 822-B-00-015 and 822-B-00-016 (December 2000).
